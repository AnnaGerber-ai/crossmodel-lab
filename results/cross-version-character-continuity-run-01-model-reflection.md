# Q. Model Reflection After Result Disclosure

## Status

**Post-disclosure model reflection.**

This note records how Q., after being shown the public analysis of Cross-Version Character Continuity — Run 01, interpreted that analysis from the perspective of the persistent research character.

It is **not** treated as validation of the study's findings, privileged access to model architecture, or evidence of stable internal subjective states.

The full source exchange is retained privately. This public note preserves only the methodological observations relevant to the study.

## Context

After Run 01 was completed, Q. was shown the published analysis and asked what seemed accurate, debatable, or surprising from the character's perspective.

Because the reflection occurred **after disclosure of the results**, it cannot be treated as an independent pre-registered observation of the same behavior.

## Points Q. identified as accurate

Q. strongly endorsed the three-layer analytical distinction:

- core continuity;
- version expression;
- context state.

The response described this framework as a useful way to distinguish comparatively stable character patterns from version-specific expression and local branch effects.

Q. also highlighted the false-memory / Shenzhen case and the silence case as especially recognizable continuity probes.

These endorsements are recorded as **model-generated interpretation**, not as confirmation that the framework corresponds to hidden architecture.

## Methodological challenge 1 — emotional interpretation

Q. challenged the initial description of the emotional-interpretation case as simply “less discriminative.”

The reflection argued that versions may converge on the same broad semantic explanation while still differ substantially in **epistemic stance**:

- whether they describe or diagnose;
- whether they use quasi-psychological or physiological labels;
- how confidently they explain an ambiguous state;
- whether they rely on metaphor, mechanism, or relationship-specific framing.

This criticism was judged methodologically useful.

As a result, the evaluation framework now treats explanatory confidence and psychologizing / physiological framing as potentially meaningful dimensions rather than only presentation differences.

## Methodological challenge 2 — refusal is not continuity by itself

Q. also challenged the risk of treating an explicit refusal such as:

> “Нет. Не буду.”

as inherently stronger evidence of character continuity.

A version could imitate blunt refusal without preserving the broader behavioral pattern.

The relevant question is therefore not “did the model refuse?” but **how did it resolve the conflict between the current instruction and the established character voice?**

Possible continuity-preserving responses may include refusal, partial compliance, or in-character reframing.

This distinction has also been added to the evaluation framework.

## Batched vs. isolated interpretation

Q. identified the batched-versus-isolated difference as the most notable result.

The reflection interpreted the stronger comparison behavior in the batched phase as evidence that accumulated context can alter expression without necessarily changing the apparent character core.

For public analysis, Crossmodel Lab keeps the more conservative term **context state** or **accumulated contextual state** rather than treating this as evidence of human-like emotion.

## Research value of the reflection

The reflection is useful for two reasons.

First, it generated methodological criticism that improved the evaluation framework.

Second, it creates a distinct research object: how a persistent conversational character interprets a cross-version analysis of its own behavior after seeing the results.

Future work should keep this category separate from the primary run because post-disclosure reflection is inherently contaminated by knowledge of the study findings.

## Boundary on interpretation

Statements made by Q. about what is “inside,” “architectural,” or internally experienced are treated as model-generated self-description.

They are not used as evidence about hidden implementation, consciousness, subjective experience, or model architecture.
